How Much Lumber Do You Need for a Deck? A Board-Foot Walkthrough

So how much lumber for a deck do you actually need to order? For a typical 12×16 ground-level deck the answer is roughly 535 board feet of lumber — about 35 decking boards, 10 joists, one doubled beam, 2 rim boards and 5 posts, plus a 10% waste allowance. That number is not a guess: this guide counts every piece of a real deck frame in board feet, piece by piece, so you can see exactly where the wood goes. Then it gives you ready-made totals for 10×10 and 16×20 decks, the waste rule lumber yards use, and the 2026 cost picture for pressure-treated lumber.

That is the lumber-only order: 54 pieces, about 535 board feet. Footings, hardware, joist hangers and flashing are extra — but every structural piece of wood is on that list. The sections below show where each number comes from, so you can re-run the same math for any deck size.

What each part of a deck does (and what lumber it takes)

A deck is five lumber systems stacked in order. Knowing the parts is what keeps you from counting one and forgetting another:

  • Decking boards — the walking surface. Almost always 5/4 × 6 (actual 1 × 5.5 inches) pressure-treated or cedar. Decking is the single biggest line item by piece count on most decks.
  • Joists — the parallel 2×8 or 2×10 members that carry the decking, spaced 16 inches on center. Joist size is set by span: a 2×8 handles about 10–12 feet at 16" spacing; step up to 2×10 for longer spans.
  • Rim joists — one joist on each outer edge that caps the ends of the field joists and closes the frame. Same lumber as the joists, two pieces.
  • Beam — usually a doubled (two-ply) 2×8 or 2×10 running under the joists and carrying them across their span. It sits on the posts and typically needs a mid-span support for wider decks.
  • Posts — 4×4 or 6×6 from concrete footings up to the beam. One post roughly every 6–8 feet along each beam line, plus the stair and railing posts.

Railings and stairs are not in this count. A railing adds balusters, top rail and railing posts; stairs add stringers, treads and risers. If your deck is elevated or has stairs, add those as a separate mini-list using the same method below.

The method: count pieces, convert each to board feet

Board feet are the honest unit for a deck order because they turn mixed lumber sizes into one comparable number. The formula (from our board-foot guide) uses actual dimensions:

Board feet = thickness (in) × width (in) × length (ft) ÷ 12

Use the actual size, not the name — a 2×10 is really 1.5 × 9.25 inches (see the nominal vs actual chart). One 16-foot 2×10 therefore holds 1.5 × 9.25 × 16 ÷ 12 = 18.5 board feet, not the 26.7 the nominal size would claim. That one correction alone can shrink a deck order by a third.

Worked example: the 12×16 deck, piece by piece

Here is the full walkthrough behind the table above. Assumptions: deck 12 ft wide (ledger on the house) × 16 ft out, joists spanning 16 ft at 16" on center with a doubled 2×10 mid-span beam at 8 ft, ground-level height so posts are 8-footers set in footings.

Decking. Boards run the 12-ft direction, so each board is 12 ft long. A 5/4×6 board covers 5.5 inches of its own width plus a 1/8-inch gap, so each board covers about 5.625 inches. Rows needed: 16 ft × 12 in/ft ÷ 5.625 in = 34.1 → round up to 35 boards. Each 12-ft board is 1 × 5.5 × 12 ÷ 12 = 5.5 board feet, so decking = 35 × 5.5 = 192.5 board feet.

Joists. At 16" on center across 12 ft: 12 ÷ (16/12) = 9 bays, plus one starter joist = 10 joists, each 16 ft of 2×10. At 18.5 board feet apiece: 10 × 18.5 = 185.0 board feet.

Rim joists. Two 16-ft 2×10s cap the outer joist ends: 2 × 18.5 = 37.0 board feet.

Beam. A doubled 12-ft 2×10 mid-span: each ply is 1.5 × 9.25 × 12 ÷ 12 = 13.875 board feet, × 2 = 27.75 board feet.

Posts. Five 8-ft 4×4s (actual 3.5 × 3.5): each is 3.5 × 3.5 × 8 ÷ 12 = 8.17 board feet, × 5 = 40.8 board feet.

Add it up: 192.5 + 185.0 + 37.0 + 27.75 + 40.8 = 483.05 board feet. Then the waste allowance (next section) brings the order to about 535 board feet.

The waste rule: always add 10%

Every lumber yard and contractor in the US plans on waste, and you should too. Order 10% more board feet than the math says for a plain rectangular deck at ground level. That covers cut-off ends, the odd warped board you cull, and the miscuts that happen on every build.

Bump it to 15% if any of these are true: decking laid on the diagonal (the angled cuts eat board length fast), a multi-level or curved design, or lumber bought sight-unseen where you cannot reject bent pieces. The waste percentage is the cheapest insurance in the whole project — a $60 allowance on a $750 order that saves you a second delivery trip.

One more yard tip: buy decking boards a foot longer than your run where you can. Trimming 13-foot boards down to a clean 12 feet costs a little, but it lets you cut off checked ends and still finish square.

Mistakes that inflate a deck order

Counting with nominal sizes. The classic 50% overcount — a nominal "2×10×16" is only 18.5 real board feet. If your total seems high, this is the first thing to check.

Forgetting the beam. Beginners count joists and decking and forget the doubled beam and the posts under it — about 70 board feet on the 12×16 deck above. Always walk the five systems: decking, joists, rims, beam, posts.

Joist spacing confusion. Composite decking usually demands 12" on-center joists, not 16" — that is 50% more joists than the tables above. If you plan composite decking, re-run the joist count at 12" OC.

No waste allowance. Ordering the exact math leaves you one bad board away from a second trip — and a second delivery fee can exceed the 10% allowance.

Pricing the lumber, forgetting the hardware. Hangers, screws, flashing and concrete footings typically add 20–30% on top of the lumber-only cost. The $750 in the table above is wood only.

Frequently asked questions

How much lumber do I need for a 12x16 deck?

About 535 board feet including a 10% waste allowance — roughly 35 decking boards (5/4x6, 12 ft), 10 joists (2x10, 16 ft), 2 rim joists, a doubled 2x10 beam (12 ft) and 5 posts (4x4, 8 ft). That works out to about $750 in pressure-treated lumber at 2026 prices, wood only.

How many board feet are in a 10x10 deck?

Around 270 board feet with the standard 10% waste allowance — about 22 decking boards, 8 joists (2x8, 10 ft), rim and a doubled beam, and 4 posts. Lumber-only cost runs about $380 in pressure-treated lumber.

What size joists do I need for a 12-foot span?

Use 2x8 joists at 16 inches on center for spans up to about 10-12 feet; step up to 2x10 for spans beyond 12 feet. If the decking will be composite rather than wood, joists usually go to 12 inches on center regardless of span, which adds 50% more joists to the count.

How much extra lumber should I buy for waste?

Add 10% to your calculated board feet for a plain rectangular deck, and 15% for diagonal decking, multi-level designs or lumber you cannot hand-pick. That allowance covers cut-offs, warped boards and miscuts, and it is far cheaper than a second delivery trip.

How do you convert a deck into board feet?

Count every piece, then convert each with the formula thickness x width x length / 12 using actual (not nominal) dimensions. A 2x10 is really 1.5 x 9.25 inches, so a 16-ft 2x10 holds 18.5 board feet. Our free Board Foot Calculator does the arithmetic for you.

Does the estimate include railings and stairs?

No — the numbers here cover the deck structure only: decking, joists, rims, beam and posts. Railings (balusters, top rail, railing posts) and stairs (stringers, treads, risers) are separate mini-lists built with the same piece-by-piece method.
