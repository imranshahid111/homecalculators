How Much Does Sod Cost Per Pallet in 2026? (By Grass Type)

Wondering about the sod cost per pallet? Expect $150–$500 per pallet of sod delivered in 2026, depending on grass type: roughly $175–$250 for Bermuda, $200–$300 for zoysia, $250–$325 for tall fescue, $275–$350 for Kentucky bluegrass, and $300–$450 for St. Augustine. A standard pallet covers 500 square feet, so one pallet handles a small backyard, while an average 2,500 sq ft lawn needs five or six pallets and a sod-only budget of about $1,200–$2,000. This guide breaks down 2026 pallet prices by grass type, what a pallet actually contains, delivery and installation math, a worked pallet-by-pallet budget for a real yard, and when buying by the roll is cheaper than the pallet.

The short answer: sod cost per pallet in 2026

Most homeowners land somewhere in the middle of the range — $225–$350 per pallet, sod only, delivered — because the big sellers are mid-priced fescues and bluegrass blends in the transition and northern zones. Three numbers set your price before you ever call a supplier:

  • Grass type: warm-season grasses grown at scale in the South (Bermuda) sit at the bottom; specialty and heavy varieties (St. Augustine, premium bluegrass blends) sit at the top.
  • Pallet size: the "standard" pallet is 500 sq ft, but some farms pack 450. A $275 quote means two different prices per square foot depending on which pallet you get.
  • Source: sod bought direct from the farm usually runs 15–30% cheaper than the same pallet stacked at a home center, which has already paid freight and markup on it.

Keep those three in mind and every quote below becomes comparable. Skip them and you will compare apples to oranges — the $190 pallet that looks cheap at 450 sq ft actually costs more per square foot than the $225 pallet at 500.

What a pallet of sod actually buys you

A pallet of sod is a wooden pallet stacked with cut rolls of grass, root layer and a thin skin of soil. The US standards you will hear:

  • Standard pallet: 50 small rolls of 2 ft × 5 ft = 500 sq ft. This is the baseline every quote below assumes.
  • Short pallet: 45 rolls (or tighter packing) = 450 sq ft. Common with some regional farms — always ask before you divide your lawn area by a pallet count.
  • Weight: 1,500–3,000 lb per pallet. This is why delivery is a flatbed or a forklift drop, not something you toss in a trunk. St. Augustine and thick-cut bluegrass sit at the heavy end.
  • Freshness window: sod starts heating and yellowing within hours of being cut. Order for delivery on (or the morning of) install day; a pallet baking in sun for two days can be half-dead before it touches soil.

The pallet is a packaging unit, not a unit of quality. Two farms' Bermuda pallets can differ in cut thickness, weed content and freshness — price per pallet is only half the comparison. Still, for budgeting, the pallet is the unit farms and most suppliers quote, so learning its math is the fastest way to read quotes.

Worked example: a 2,500 sq ft lawn, pallet by pallet

Take a typical suburban lawn with 2,500 sq ft of plantable grass (after subtracting the house, driveway and beds — if you haven't measured yet, the 10-minute measuring method walks you through it). Add 7% waste for curves and bed edges: 2,500 × 1.07 = 2,675 sq ft to order. At 500 sq ft per pallet, that's 5.35 → 6 pallets, pallets being whole units.

Now price it at a mid-range tall fescue, $300 per delivered pallet:

The professional figure includes grading, the sod, laying and cleanup — and it is why the "sod cost per pallet" question is only the material half of most budgets. On this yard, the pallet quotes ($1,800) are less than half of the installed price ($4,000). If you are comparing contractor bids, ask each one to split material from labor: a $4,500 bid with $1,800 of material is $2,700 of labor, and that is the number you can actually negotiate or do yourself.

Swap the grass and the pallet math changes the whole budget. The same six pallets in Bermuda at $200 each = $1,200 of sod (saving $600); in St. Augustine at $400 each = $2,400 (costing $600 more). For a budget-sensitive project, grass type is often a bigger decision than shaving delivery fees.

How pallet pricing is built: farm, freight, markup

A pallet's price is three stacked costs, and knowing the stack tells you where the discount is hiding:

  • Farm gate price (~60–70%): the farm's cost to grow, cut, stack and load. This is the cheapest place to buy sod, period — direct farm sales skip everything below.
  • Freight (~15–25%): the flatbed to your curb. Farms charge per load, not per pallet, so one six-pallet load has dramatically cheaper per-pallet freight than two three-pallet loads on different days. Order once, for the whole job.
  • Retailer markup (~15–25%): what a home center or landscape supply adds for stocking, storing (sod is dying inventory — literally) and selling pallets all week. Convenient, pricier.

Practical takeaway: if you can take a full flatbed delivery from a farm on install day, that is almost always the cheapest legitimate price. Home-center pallets make sense for small jobs (see the roll math below), urgent replacements, or when the farm's minimum order is bigger than your yard.

When buying by the roll beats the pallet

Not every lawn is pallet-sized. Sod by the roll at home centers runs about $4–$9 per 10 sq ft roll ($0.40–$0.90/sq ft) — a premium over the farm pallet rate, but with no minimum and no waste from rounding up to whole pallets.

*The asterisk matters: at 250 rolls, no home center has that much fresh stock of one grass on hand — you would be buying across batches and days, which risks color mismatch and wilted rolls. The rule of thumb: under ~800 sq ft, buy rolls; over it, buy pallets. And check the fine print on St. Augustine farms — some sell pallet-only, no partial pallets and no single rolls, which forces the pallet even on small jobs.

Five ways people overpay for pallet sod

Comparing quotes without the pallet size. A $260 quote on a 450 sq ft pallet is $0.58/sq ft; a $285 quote on a 500 sq ft pallet is $0.57/sq ft. The "cheaper" quote is the expensive one. Always convert to price per square foot.

Ordering at peak spring without a price lock. April–May demand lifts prices 10–20% in many markets. If your project can wait, late-summer and early-fall quotes from farms are often softer — and fall is actually good planting weather for cool-season grasses.

Splitting delivery across days. Freight is per load. Two three-pallet deliveries on separate days can double the freight line versus one six-pallet load — schedule prep so the full order arrives the day you lay it.

Paying for pallets they didn't measure for. Ordering for the lot instead of the lawn buys one to two pallets of grass that never gets laid. Measure the plantable area first (see the measuring guide), then convert.

Ignoring the freshness check. A cheap pallet is expensive if a third of it is heat-damaged. Before the truck leaves: the rolls should be cool to the touch, green at the cut edges, and the soil layer moist. Reject cooked pallets on the spot — replacing them mid-job costs a second delivery fee.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a pallet of sod cost in 2026?

A pallet of sod costs $150–$500 delivered in 2026 depending on grass type: roughly $175–$250 for Bermuda, $200–$300 for zoysia, $250–$325 for tall fescue, $275–$350 for Kentucky bluegrass, and $300–$450 for St. Augustine. Most homeowners pay $225–$350 per pallet delivered.

How many square feet does a pallet of sod cover?

A standard pallet covers 500 square feet (50 rolls of 2x5 ft). Some farms pack 450 square feet per pallet — confirm the coverage with your supplier before converting your lawn area into pallets.

How many pallets of sod do I need for 2,500 square feet?

For 2,500 square feet of plantable lawn, add 7% waste (2,675 sq ft to order) and divide by 500 sq ft per pallet: 5.35, rounded up to 6 pallets. Budget about $1,200–$2,000 for sod only at 2026 prices, plus $75–$150 delivery.

Why is St. Augustine sod more expensive per pallet?

St. Augustine rolls are heavier and thicker-cut, cost more to grow and ship, and many farms sell it pallet-only with no partials or single rolls — all of which push the pallet price to $300–$450, the top of the 2026 range.

Is it cheaper to buy sod by the pallet or by the roll?

By the roll is cheaper for jobs under about 800 square feet (30–80 rolls at $4–$9 each) because you avoid rounding up to whole pallets. Above ~800 sq ft, the farm pallet price per square foot wins, and roll stock becomes hard to source in one fresh batch.

How much does sod installation add per pallet?

Professional installation roughly triples the material cost: installed sod runs $1.00–$2.00 per square foot all-in, so a $300 pallet (500 sq ft) lands around $500–$1,000 installed including grading and cleanup. Always ask contractors to split material from labor in the bid.
