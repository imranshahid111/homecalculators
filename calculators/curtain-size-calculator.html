Curtain Size Calculator

Panel width, fullness and length — curtains that look tailored, not skimpy.

Calculate

Measure the rod itself if you have one — it is usually wider than the window.
Ready-made panels are commonly 50–54 in wide (flat, unpleated).
Standard lengths: 63, 84, 96, 108 in. Measure from rod to where hems should end.

A worked example

Example: A 72-inch rod with standard 2× fullness needs 144 inches of fabric width. With 54-inch panels that is 3 panels — most people buy 4 for a symmetric pair each side. An 84-inch drop is the standard ready-made length for rod-to-sill-to-floor on a typical window.

The formula

Total Width Needed = Rod Width × Fullness. Panels = Total Width ÷ Panel Width (round up). Fullness is the gather ratio: 1.5× looks flat and casual, 2× is the standard tailored look, 2.5× gives deep formal folds. Never plan 1× — flat curtains look like a bed sheet.

How to get an accurate result

The most common curtain mistake is buying panels that add up to exactly the window width. Hung flat like that, curtains look starved and cheap, because real drapery is gathered — the folds are what make it look finished. That gather is the fullness ratio in this calculator, and 2× is the number most decorators start from: a 72-inch rod wants about 144 inches of total panel width across all panels.

Measure the rod, not the window. Rods are normally mounted 4–6 inches beyond the window frame on each side so the open curtains clear most of the glass and let in maximum light. If you have no rod yet, measure the window width and add 8–12 inches for the rod length. Width is measured with panels laid flat and unpleated — a "54-inch panel" is 54 inches of flat fabric, which covers only about 27 inches of rod at 2× fullness.

Length is the second decision. The classic options: sill length (ends at the windowsill, casual, good for kitchens), apron length (a few inches below the sill), floor length (hem just kisses the floor — the most popular tailored look), and puddle length (extra fabric pooling on the floor, formal and impractical with pets). Measure from the top of the rod to your chosen ending point, and remember that rod-pocket and tab-top curtains lose a few inches differently than grommet or ring styles.

Ready-made panels in the USA come in standard widths (mostly 50–54 in) and lengths (63, 84, 96, 108 in), usually $15–60 per panel depending on fabric and lining. If your calculated length falls between sizes, buy the longer panel and hem it — hemming is a $10–15 job at a tailor and far better than curtains hovering two inches above the floor.

Step by step

  1. Measure the rod width (or window width + 8–12 in if no rod yet).
  2. Choose fullness: 2× for the standard look.
  3. Enter the flat width of the panels you are considering.
  4. Round panel count up — to an even number for paired hanging.
  5. Pick the standard length at or just above your measured drop; hem if needed.

Frequently asked questions

What size curtains do I need for a 72-inch window?

With a rod of about 80–84 in and 2× fullness, you need ~160–168 in of total panel width — typically 4 panels of 54 in (2 per side), in your chosen length such as 84 or 96 in.

How wide should curtains be compared to the window?

Total flat panel width should be 1.5–2.5 times the rod width. 2× is the standard recommendation; less looks flat, more looks formal.

Should curtains touch the floor?

The most popular look is the hem just touching or hovering 1/2 inch above the floor. Shorter apron/sill lengths suit kitchens and bathrooms.

How high above the window should I hang curtains?

Mount the rod 4–6 inches above the frame (or halfway to the ceiling) and 4–6 inches wider on each side — it makes the window look larger and lets curtains clear the glass when open.
