Asphalt Calculator

How many tons of asphalt does your driveway or patch need? Get the tonnage right.

Calculate

Driveways: 2.5–3 in surface over base. Parking areas: 4 in+.

A worked example

Example: A 50 ft × 12 ft driveway at 3 inches compacted thickness is 600 sq ft and 150 cu ft of asphalt — at the standard density of 145 lb/cu ft that is 10.9 tons. Order about 12 tons with a waste allowance.

The formula

Tons = Area (sq ft) × Thickness (ft) × Density (lb/cu ft) ÷ 2,000. Standard hot-mix asphalt weighs about 145 lb per cubic foot compacted (roughly 2 tons per cubic yard at typical depths). A handy cross-check: 1 ton covers about 55 sq ft at 3 inches deep.

How to get an accurate result

Asphalt is ordered by the ton, but driveways are measured in feet and inches — this conversion is where most ordering mistakes happen. The single most common error is forgetting that thickness is in inches while everything else is in feet: 3 inches is 0.25 ft, and missing that factor makes an order four times too big or too small. The calculator handles the conversion, but the cross-check is worth remembering: one ton of hot mix covers roughly 55 sq ft at 3 inches, 80 sq ft at 2 inches, or 40 sq ft at 4 inches.

Density matters more than people expect. Hot-mix asphalt from the plant compacts to about 140–150 lb per cubic foot depending on the aggregate. If your paving contractor quotes tonnage noticeably higher than this calculator at the same thickness, ask which density they are using — or whether the quote quietly includes a thicker section. For resurfacing quotes, also confirm whether the thickness quoted is compacted thickness (what you end up with) or loose thickness before rolling.

Think about the base, not just the surface. Asphalt is a flexible pavement: a 3-inch layer over a well-compacted 4–6 inch gravel base will outlast a 4-inch layer laid over soft soil. Most premature driveway failures — rutting, alligator cracking, edges breaking away — are base failures, not asphalt failures. If you are budgeting a full replacement, gravel tonnage and excavation often cost as much as the asphalt itself.

Material-only hot mix in the USA typically costs about $80–150 per ton from the plant, varying with oil prices and region; installed residential driveways commonly run $5–10 per sq ft including base work. Hot mix also has a minimum practical load — plants and pavers may charge a short-load fee under about 5 tons, which is why small patches are often done with cold patch instead despite its shorter life.

Step by step

  1. Measure the paved area in feet (split odd shapes into rectangles).
  2. Choose compacted thickness: 2 in resurface, 3 in new driveway typical.
  3. Use 145 lb/cu ft density unless your plant specifies otherwise.
  4. Add 10% for edges, spillage and low spots.
  5. Confirm the plant's minimum load and delivery window before ordering.

Frequently asked questions

How many tons of asphalt do I need for a driveway?

A typical 50×12 ft driveway at 3 in thick needs about 11 tons — order 12 with waste. Enter your own dimensions for an exact figure.

How much area does 1 ton of asphalt cover?

About 55 sq ft at 3 inches compacted, 80 sq ft at 2 inches, or 40 sq ft at 4 inches, using standard 145 lb/cu ft hot mix.

How thick should a driveway asphalt layer be?

2.5–3 inches compacted over a compacted gravel base for cars. Heavier vehicles or poor soil call for 4 inches or more.

What does asphalt cost per ton?

Roughly $80–$150 per ton for hot mix material in the USA, before delivery and installation. Installed driveways usually total $5–10 per sq ft.
