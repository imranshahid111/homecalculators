Paver Calculator

Pavers, gravel base and sand for your patio or walkway — calculated in seconds.

Calculate

Common brick paver: 4 × 8 in. Large slab: 12 × 12 or 16 × 16 in.

A worked example

Example: A 10 ft × 10 ft patio is 100 sq ft. With standard 4×8 in brick pavers (0.22 sq ft each) you need 450 pavers exactly, or 495 with 10% extra for cuts. The base takes about 1.23 cu yd of gravel and 0.31 cu yd of bedding sand.

The formula

Pavers = Patio Area ÷ Paver Area. Paver area in sq ft = (length in × width in) ÷ 144. Base: a standard patio sits on 4 inches of compacted gravel (area × 4/12 ÷ 27 = cubic yards) plus 1 inch of bedding sand. Driveways need a 6–8 inch base instead.

How to get an accurate result

The pavers themselves are only part of the shopping list. A patio that lasts is built like a sandwich: excavated soil, landscape fabric, 4 inches of compacted gravel base, 1 inch of coarse bedding sand, the pavers, edge restraints, and finally polymeric sand swept into the joints. Skipping the base is the number one reason patios sink and wobble within two seasons — the calculator includes the base materials so your order is complete the first time.

Paver sizes are nominal too. A "4×8" brick paver is usually very close to that size, but manufacturers intend a small sand joint between pavers, so the laid coverage per paver is slightly larger than the bare brick. That is why this calculator uses exact paver dimensions for the count and then relies on your waste percentage to absorb the difference — for simple square patios in a running-bond pattern, 10% is comfortable; for diagonal (45°) patterns or curved edges, use 15%.

Buy all pavers from the same production lot if you can. Color blends vary slightly between lots, and a patio patched with a visibly different shade will bother you every time you look at it. Keep a handful of spares in the garage afterward — individual pavers crack or stain over the years, and a perfect match years later is unlikely.

Material costs in the USA: concrete pavers run roughly $3–6 per sq ft, gravel about $40–60 per cubic yard delivered, and bedding sand $30–50 per yard. For the 100 sq ft example patio, expect roughly $450–750 in materials before edging and polymeric sand — usually half or less of what a contractor would charge for the same job installed.

Step by step

  1. Measure the patio area; sketch curves as rectangles and add them.
  2. Enter your paver's actual length and width in inches.
  3. Set waste: 10% straight patterns, 15% diagonal or curved.
  4. Note the gravel and sand quantities for your base order.
  5. Order pavers from one lot and keep a few spares.

Frequently asked questions

How many pavers do I need for a 10×10 patio?

With 4×8 in pavers, 450 exactly — buy about 495 to cover cuts. With 12×12 in pavers, 100 exactly, about 110 with extra. Use the calculator with your exact paver size.

How deep should a paver base be?

4 inches of compacted gravel plus 1 inch of bedding sand for patios and walkways. Driveways need 6–8 inches of base because of vehicle weight.

How much gravel do I need under pavers?

About 0.0123 cubic yards per square foot at 4 inches deep — roughly 1.25 cu yd for a 100 sq ft patio, or about 1.7 tons.

Should I use polymeric sand between pavers?

Yes for most patios. It hardens after misting, locks pavers together, resists weeds and ants far better than plain sand, and only needs refreshing every few years.
