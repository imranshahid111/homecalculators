Sod Calculator

How many rolls or pallets of sod does your yard need? Measure, enter, done.

Calculate

A worked example

Example: A 40 ft × 25 ft backyard is 1,000 sq ft. With 10% extra for cutting you need 1,100 sq ft of sod — that is 110 rolls, or 3 pallets at 450 sq ft per pallet (you round up, because pallets are sold whole).

The formula

Area = Length × Width. A standard sod roll covers about 10 sq ft (2 ft × 5 ft). A pallet typically covers 400–500 sq ft depending on the farm. Rolls = Area ÷ 10; Pallets = Area ÷ pallet coverage. Always add 5–10% for cuts around curves, trees and edges.

How to get an accurate result

Sod is sold two ways: by the roll for small patches and by the pallet for whole yards. A roll is usually 2 feet by 5 feet — 10 square feet — while a pallet holds 400 to 500 square feet depending on the sod farm and the grass type. Because farms differ, always confirm the pallet coverage before you order; a 50 sq ft difference per pallet adds up fast on a big yard.

Measure your yard in sections if it is not a simple rectangle. Break an L-shaped lawn into two rectangles, calculate each, and add them together. For a circular area, measure the diameter and use radius × radius × 3.14. Subtract large areas that will not be sodded — patios, driveways, sheds and planting beds — but do not subtract small obstacles like trees; the extra sod simply gets cut around them, which is exactly what your waste allowance covers.

Order sod for delivery the same day you plan to lay it, ideally in the morning. Sod is a living product: rolls left stacked on a pallet in summer heat can yellow within 24–48 hours. Prepare the soil first — grade it about 1 inch below paths and driveways, rake it smooth, and lightly water it — so the sod goes straight from pallet to ground. Lay the sod the same day and water deeply.

Retail pallet prices in the USA typically run about $150–$300 per pallet depending on grass type (fescue and bluegrass at the lower end, zoysia and Bermuda higher in many regions), plus a delivery fee of $50–$150 that is often waived on multi-pallet orders. Getting one extra pallet is cheaper than a second delivery if you come up short.

Step by step

  1. Measure the length and width of each lawn section in feet.
  2. Multiply to get square feet; add sections together.
  3. Choose your farm's pallet coverage (ask if unsure — 450 is standard).
  4. Add 10% for cutting around edges and curves.
  5. Order delivery for the morning you will lay it.

Frequently asked questions

How much sod do I need for 1,000 square feet?

At least 1,000 sq ft of sod — 100 rolls or about 2.2 pallets at 450 sq ft each. With a 10% cutting allowance, order 1,100 sq ft, which rounds to 3 pallets.

How many rolls of sod are on a pallet?

A pallet holds roughly 40–50 rolls (400–500 sq ft total), depending on the farm and roll size. Always confirm coverage per pallet when you order.

How much does a pallet of sod cost?

Typically $150–$300 per pallet in the USA depending on grass species and region, plus delivery. Zoysia and specialty grasses cost more than fescue or bluegrass.

Can sod sit on the pallet overnight?

Try to avoid it. Lay sod the day it arrives — in hot weather rolls begin to heat up and yellow within a day. If you must wait, unroll a few rolls in shade and keep them moist.
