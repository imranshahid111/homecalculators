A worked example

Example: A roof that rises 6 inches over a 12-inch run is a 6/12 pitch — an angle of 26.6°, a 50% slope, and every square foot of flat attic floor under it corresponds to about 1.118 sq ft of actual roof surface.

The formula

Pitch = Rise ÷ Run, expressed over 12. Angle = arctan(Rise ÷ Run). The roof-area multiplier is √(Rise² + Run²) ÷ Run — multiply the building's flat footprint by it to get true roofing area for shingles and underlayment.

How to get an accurate result

Pitch is the language of roofing. In the USA it is written as rise over a standard 12-inch run — 4/12, 6/12, 9/12 — while much of the rest of the world uses degrees or percent slope. The three describe the same thing: a 6/12 pitch is 26.6° and a 50% slope. Confusing percent with pitch is a classic mistake — a 100% slope is 45°, which is a steep 12/12 pitch, not a vertical wall of "100 pitch."

The safest way to measure pitch is from inside the attic or with a level against a rafter: rest a 12-inch level horizontally, then measure straight down from the 12-inch mark to the rafter — that vertical distance is your rise. Measuring off a ladder at the gable end works too. Phone inclinometer apps on the roof surface are fine for estimates but can be off by a degree or two, which matters when you are near a material limit.

Pitch decides what roofing you may install. Standard asphalt shingles need at least 4/12 (2/12–4/12 requires double underlayment or ice-and-water shield underneath), metal panels typically need 3/12 or more depending on seam type, and flat-roof membranes take over below 2/12. Pitch also drives price: steep roofs (9/12 and up) cost more to install because roofers need staging, harnesses and more material handling time.

For material estimates, never order from the flat footprint. Multiply the footprint of each roof section by the area multiplier from this calculator — a 6/12 roof already needs about 12% more shingles than its footprint suggests, and a 12/12 needs 41% more. Then add the usual 10–15% for waste, cuts at valleys and hips, and starter and ridge courses.

Step by step

  1. Measure rise over a 12 in horizontal run (attic rafter + level is safest).
  2. Enter rise and run (run = 12 for standard notation).
  3. Read pitch, angle and slope percent.
  4. Use the area multiplier on flat footprint to size material orders.
  5. Check your roofing material's minimum pitch before choosing it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 4/12 pitch in degrees?

4/12 pitch is 18.4 degrees. For reference: 6/12 is 26.6°, 9/12 is 36.9°, and 12/12 is exactly 45°.

How do I measure my roof pitch without climbing on it?

From the attic: hold a 12-inch level horizontally against a rafter and measure down from its end to the rafter — that is the rise over 12. Gable-end measurement from a ladder also works.

What roof pitch is walkable?

Most roofers walk up to about 6/12 comfortably, use roof jacks and planks at 7/12–9/12, and need staging and harnesses above that. Treat any roof work with proper fall protection.

What is the minimum pitch for shingles?

Standard asphalt shingles need 4/12 or steeper. Between 2/12 and 4/12 they require special underlayment; below 2/12 use a flat-roof membrane system instead.
