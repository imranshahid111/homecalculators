A worked example

Example: A 15,000-gallon pool testing at 2,000 ppm with a 3,200 ppm target is short 1,200 ppm. That needs 15,000 × 1,200 × 8.34 ÷ 1,000,000 = 150 lbs of salt — four 40 lb bags (buy the 4th bag, use what you need, keep the rest for top-ups).

The formula

Salt (lbs) = Gallons × (Target ppm − Current ppm) × 8.34 ÷ 1,000,000. The 8.34 is the weight in pounds of one gallon of water; ppm is parts per million by weight, so the formula converts a ppm shortfall into pounds of salt for your pool's exact volume.

How to get an accurate result

Saltwater pools are not salt-free and they are not maintenance-free — the salt cell converts dissolved salt into chlorine, and the salt itself is barely consumed. Levels drop mainly when water leaves the pool: splash-out, backwashing, leaks and draining for winter all carry salt away, and rain and refill water dilute what remains. That is why most pools need a few bags each spring and occasional small top-ups, not a full recharge.

Test before you add. Chlorinator displays are convenient but drift as the cell ages or scales up, and they can read 300–500 ppm low. A liquid salt test kit or fresh test strips give a more honest number. Also check your manual before choosing a target: different chlorinators specify different ranges (commonly 2,700–3,400 ppm), and running far above the range can trigger high-salt shutdowns and is hard on heaters and metal fittings.

Technique matters more than speed. Brush any undissolved piles so they do not sit on the plaster, add salt in the shallow end with the pump running and the salt cell temporarily off, and let it circulate a full 24 hours before retesting. Adding only about 80% of the calculated amount, dissolving, retesting and topping up avoids the classic overshoot — because the only cure for too much salt is draining and refilling.

Pool salt is just high-purity sodium chloride without anti-caking additives — a 40 lb bag typically costs $7–12 in the USA. Do not substitute rock salt, table salt (iodized, and may contain anti-caking agents that cloud water), or water-softener pellets with additives. Store spare bags in a dry place; salt absorbs moisture and turns into one solid brick otherwise.

Step by step

  1. Find your pool volume in gallons (or compute L × W × avg depth × 7.48).
  2. Test the current salt level with a kit or strip, not just the cell display.
  3. Enter your chlorinator's target ppm (usually ~3,200).
  4. Add 80% of the calculated salt, circulate 24 hours, retest.
  5. Top up with the remainder if still below target.

Frequently asked questions

How much salt does a 20,000 gallon pool need?

From zero salt at a 3,200 ppm target: about 534 lbs, or 14 bags of 40 lb. From a typical spring level of 2,400 ppm it needs only about 134 lbs — roughly 3–4 bags.

What happens if I put too much salt in my pool?

The water can taste salty, the chlorinator may shut down on a high-salt fault, and corrosion risk rises. There is no chemical neutralizer — partially drain and refill with fresh water to dilute.

How long after adding salt can I swim?

Wait until it has fully dissolved and circulated — typically 24 hours — and keep the salt cell off during that period so it does not read the temporary spike.

Why does my pool keep losing salt?

Salt only leaves with water: splash-out, backwashing, leaks and rain overflow. If you lose salt much faster than water, suspect a leak.
