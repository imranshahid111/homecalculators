Fence Material Calculator

A worked example

Example: A 100 ft privacy fence with 8 ft post spacing has 13 sections and 14 posts, 26 rails (2 per section), and — with 5.5 in pickets and a 1/4 in gap — about 209 pickets, plus roughly 28 bags of concrete.

The formula

Posts = (Length ÷ Spacing) + 1. Rails = sections × rails per section. Pickets = (Length in inches) ÷ (picket width + gap). This counts one straight run — for multiple sides, calculate each side separately and add them, because every corner and end needs its own post.

How to get an accurate result

A fence is really a series of sections between posts, and every material count flows from the section count. Posts are the structure: set them a third of their length deep (a 6 ft fence needs an 8 ft post with 2 ft in the ground), in concrete, with the crown of the concrete sloped away from the post so water sheds instead of pooling and rotting the wood.

Picket math surprises people — a 100 ft fence needs over 200 pickets. Buy about 5% more than the calculator shows, because a few pickets in every bundle are warped, split or ugly, and on a privacy fence the gaps matter: freshly installed pressure-treated pickets shrink as they dry, so a fence built board-tight in spring will show slim gaps by fall. Many builders deliberately leave a 1/4 inch gap for exactly this reason.

Calculate each side of the yard separately rather than entering one total length. Corners, gates and ends each terminate a run, and each run rounds up its own sections — combining lengths first under-counts posts. Gates are not calculated here: a standard gate needs its own heavier posts, a frame and hardware, so budget it as a separate small project.

Typical USA material costs for a DIY wood privacy fence run about $12–25 per linear foot all-in (posts, rails, pickets, concrete, screws and hardware), versus $30–60 per foot installed by a contractor. Pressure-treated pine is the budget standard; cedar costs roughly 30–50% more but resists rot and insects better and stays straighter.

Step by step

  1. Measure each fence run separately, in feet.
  2. Choose post spacing: 8 ft standard, 6 ft for windy sites or heavy gates.
  3. Enter picket width and the gap you plan to leave.
  4. Add up posts, rails, pickets and concrete for all runs.
  5. Buy 5% extra pickets for warped or split boards.

Frequently asked questions

How many pickets do I need for a 100 ft fence?

With standard 5.5 in pickets and a 1/4 in gap, about 209 pickets for 100 ft — plus a few spares for rejects. Tighter gaps or narrower pickets increase the count.

How far apart should fence posts be?

8 feet is standard for wood privacy fences. Use 6 feet in windy areas, on slopes, or where the fence will carry a heavy gate.

How much concrete does each fence post need?

About one to two 60 lb bags per post for a typical 24–30 in deep hole. Deeper or wider holes in soft soil need more.

How deep should fence posts be set?

At least one-third of the post length, and below the frost line in cold climates. A 6 ft fence typically uses 8 ft posts buried 2 ft deep.
