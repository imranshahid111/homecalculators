Drywall Calculator

Sheets, screws, mud and tape for a room — no mid-project hardware store runs.

A worked example

Example: A 12×12 ft room with 8 ft ceilings has 384 sq ft of wall and 144 sq ft of ceiling. Minus a door and two windows (51 sq ft), the net is 477 sq ft — 15 sheets of 4×8 drywall, about 600 screws, 2 buckets of compound and 1 roll of tape.

The formula

Wall area = 2 × (L + W) × Height; Ceiling = L × W. Subtract about 21 sq ft per standard door and 15 sq ft per average window. Sheets = Net Area ÷ Sheet Area (32 for 4×8). Screws average about 40 per sheet; a 4.5-gallon bucket of joint compound finishes roughly 400 sq ft.

How to get an accurate result

Running out of drywall mid-room is more than an annoyance — sheets from a second trip can be a slightly different thickness batch, and stopping mid-ceiling leaves a cold joint that is hard to finish invisibly. That is why estimators calculate the true net area (walls plus ceiling, minus real openings) instead of guessing from floor area, and then round up to whole sheets. Offcuts from around doors and windows get reused above closets and in small wall sections, so true waste on a rectangular room is usually only a few percent.

Sheet size is a real decision. 4×8 sheets are easiest to transport and handle alone, but on a big room 4×12 sheets mean fewer seams to tape and finish — and finishing is where most of the labor hides. Ceilings especially benefit from longer sheets. The trade-off is weight: a 4×12 sheet of 1/2-inch drywall weighs about 75 lb and is a two-person lift, ideally with a rented drywall lift for ceilings.

Use the right thickness: 1/2 inch is standard for walls, 5/8 inch fire-rated for garage walls and ceilings under living space in many codes, and 1/4 inch flexible sheets for curves and for going over existing surfaces. Moisture-resistant board (green or purple) belongs in bathrooms — though never inside a shower surround, which needs cement board instead.

Budget-wise, drywall is one of the cheaper finishes: in the USA a 4×8 sheet of 1/2-inch typically costs $12–18, screws a few cents each, and compound about $18–25 per bucket. For the example 12×12 room, materials land around $300–400 — while having the same room hung, taped and finished professionally often runs $1,500–2,500, which is why drywall is such a popular DIY project.

Step by step

  1. Measure room length, width and ceiling height.
  2. Pick your sheet size (4×8 is the easy-handling standard).
  3. Enter doors and windows so the area is net, not gross.
  4. Buy sheets rounded up, plus screws, compound and tape from the list.
  5. Add one spare sheet if this is your first time hanging.

Frequently asked questions

How many sheets of drywall do I need for a 12×12 room?

About 15 sheets of 4×8 for walls plus ceiling with typical door/window deductions — 12 sheets for walls only. Enter your exact openings in the calculator for a tighter count.

How many screws are in a sheet of drywall?

Roughly 32–48 depending on stud spacing and whether it is a wall or ceiling. This calculator uses about 40 per sheet, which matches common field practice.

How much joint compound do I need?

A 4.5-gallon bucket of ready-mix finishes about 350–400 sq ft of drywall through all coats. The example room needs about 2 buckets.

Should I use 4×8 or 4×12 sheets?

4×12 sheets mean fewer seams and faster finishing on large rooms, but they are heavy and hard to move through halls and stairs. Most DIYers are happier with 4×8.
