Pond Volume Calculator

Gallons in your pond — for the right pump, filter, liner and treatment doses.

Calculate

Measure a few spots and average them — most ponds slope.

A worked example

Example: A rectangular 10 ft × 8 ft pond averaging 2.5 ft deep holds 10 × 8 × 2.5 × 7.48 = 1,496 gallons — so it wants a pump rated at least ~1,500 GPH and, with overlap, roughly a 17 ft × 15 ft liner.

The formula

Gallons = Length × Width × Average Depth × 7.48 × Shape Factor. Use factor 1.0 for rectangular ponds, 0.785 for oval/round, and about 0.7 for kidney shapes. Liner size = dimension + (2 × depth) + 2 ft for overlap, on both length and width.

How to get an accurate result

Almost every pond decision runs on gallons: pump size, filter rating, how many fish the pond can support, and the dose of every water treatment and medication. Guessing the volume of an irregular pond is notoriously unreliable — most owners overestimate by a wide margin, then oversize equipment and overdose treatments. Five minutes with a tape measure and this calculator beats any guess.

Depth is where the error hides. Ponds slope, have planting shelves, and settle. Measure depth in several places — shallow shelf, middle, deepest point — and average them honestly. Rocks, gravel and large plant baskets displace water too; a heavily rocked pond may hold 10–20% less than its bare dimensions suggest. For treatment dosing, knowing you are within 10% is what matters, not false precision.

For pumps, the rule of thumb is to circulate the full pond volume at least once per hour for a fish pond (twice per hour for heavily stocked koi), remembering that a pump's rated GPH is at zero lift — real flow drops as you push water up to a waterfall or filter. Check the pump's flow chart at your actual head height, and size the filter for at least 1.5× the pond volume if fish are planned.

Liner sizing follows the formula in the results: length plus twice the depth plus 2 ft of overlap, and the same for width. EPDM rubber liner is the usual choice in the USA at roughly $0.80–1.20 per sq ft. Buying one size up is cheap insurance — excess liner trims off in minutes, but a liner that comes up 6 inches short at the edge is a rebuild.

Step by step

  1. Measure the longest length and widest width at water level.
  2. Measure depth in 3–4 spots and average them.
  3. Pick the shape closest to yours for the correction factor.
  4. Note gallons for pump/filter sizing and treatment doses.
  5. Use the suggested liner size when ordering — round up one size.

Frequently asked questions

How many gallons is a 10×8 pond?

At an average depth of 2.5 ft, a rectangular 10×8 pond holds about 1,500 gallons. At 3 ft deep it is about 1,800 gallons.

What size pump do I need for my pond?

At minimum, a pump that turns over the full volume once per hour at your actual lift height — a 1,500-gallon fish pond wants roughly a 1,500+ GPH pump after head loss.

How do I measure a pond liner size?

Liner length = pond length + (2 × max depth) + 2 ft; same for width. Use maximum depth here, not average, so the liner reaches the bottom with overlap to spare.

How many fish can my pond hold?

A common guide is 1 inch of fish per 10 gallons for goldfish-type stocking with good filtration — a 1,500-gallon pond suits about 150 inches of fish. Koi need far more room: at least 250 gallons per adult koi.
